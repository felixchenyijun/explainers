Bees, ants & wasps · Ch. 10

Why raise sisters instead of daughters?

A haploid father passes the same genetic set to every daughter. A diploid mother passes a shuffled half. Full sisters therefore share, on average, ¾ by descent; mother and daughter share ½.

Helping rear reproductive sisters can transmit shared variants indirectly. A worker need not reproduce personally.

The ¾ calculation assumes one mother and the same father, with unrelated parents. Honeybee queens often mate multiply. Relatedness alone does not establish the payoff: benefits, costs, brothers, and ecology also matter.

Bird alarm calls · Chs. 1, 10

A warning can help copies in other bodies.

A call warns nearby birds. If it carries a cost, helping relatives could offset that cost. But calling may also protect the caller or its mate.

The behavior is observed. Kin selection is one candidate explanation; the call alone does not prove it.

Ant sex allocation · Ch. 10

A prediction you can weigh.

In the simple single-queen, single-mating model, workers favor 3:1 female:male investment; the queen favors 1:1. Workers are related by ¾ to full sisters but ¼ to brothers; the queen is equally related to both sexes.

Trivers & Hare reported investment near 3:1 across 20 monogynous ant species. This is quantitative support for a prediction, not just a plausible story.

02

Shared genes do not mean identical interests.

Families cooperate, but interests overlap only partly. And outside the family, winning does not always mean fighting.

03

Helping can pay when partners meet again.

Unrelated partners need a different route: today’s cost can buy future benefits, provided cooperation survives exploitation.

Sucker

Grooms anyone; vulnerable to exploitation.

Cheat

Grudger

Vampire bats & Tit for Tat · Ch. 12*

A model, then a real-world parallel.

Tit for Tat starts by cooperating, then copies its partner’s previous move. Axelrod’s tournaments showed how conditional cooperation can succeed when encounters repeat. It is not a universal best strategy.

Vampire bats regurgitate blood to hungry roostmates. Wilkinson’s study linked sharing to relatedness and opportunities for reciprocation, supporting both kinship and reciprocity.

Bats are not proven to run Tit for Tat. Real relationships are more complex than a tournament. *Chapter 12 was added in 1989.

For each animal, ask: which inherited tendency would leave more copies—and through whom?

The examples support mechanisms and predictions within a gene-centered account. They are not independent proofs that every trait has one adaptive explanation, or that selection at other levels is impossible. Evolutionary explanation does not prescribe how humans should behave.
