01 / WHY THE CLOCKS SEPARATE

The ice has
a head start.

Snow does not become sealed ice overnight. In the porous layer called firn, air can still exchange with the atmosphere after the snow has fallen. By the time that air is enclosed, the surrounding ice is already older.

Explanatory drawing · not to scale

One highlighted layer, three conceptual stages. No precise duration, bubble path, or sealing depth is implied. Trapped air represents an age distribution, summarized here by a modeled gas age.

Ice: water isotopes can inform a temperature reconstructionAir: gases record atmospheric composition

Neither is a thermometer reading from the distant past. To compare an isotope record with a gas record, researchers need compatible chronologies, the actual measurement depths, and a policy for their different sampling resolutions. A common depth alone cannot align them in time.

02 / AN UNEXPECTED COUNTEREXAMPLE

Uncertain dates.
A clearer difference.

At the deepest level in this table, the ice and air age bands overlap. That does not mean their age difference is zero. Both clocks belong to the same dating model, so some uncertainty moves them together.

About ±5.8 thousand years on either date.
±570 years on the gap.

The source provides a covariance-aware uncertainty for the difference. Adding the two errors as if they were independent would answer the wrong question.

These are not climate-response lags. The ice–air age gap is a consequence of how the archive forms. The depth gap tells you where equal-time material sits in the core now; it is not the original firn sealing depth. Neither quantity, on its own, tells you whether CO₂ or temperature changed first.

Sources, precision & what this page leaves out

The archive

Bouchet et al. (2026), AICC2023, EDC, PANGAEA 990377. Version 2, 5 February 2026; CC BY 4.0. All 5,806 depth rows are included. The 85,573 “data points” in its metadata count populated fields, not independent samples. The earlier 961018 edition had errors in its AICC2012 ages.

The model and its units

Bouchet et al. (2023), Climate of the Past, and the archived Paleochrono experiment. Its output confirms that the generic ± column for ice–gas age uncertainty is in thousands of years in the PANGAEA table. Delta-depth uncertainty is in metres. Both are published 1σ (one standard deviation) model uncertainties, not independent-error sums.

Matching and display

A selection is an exact depth-grid row. To match its gas age, this page inverts the strictly increasing ice-age grid by linear interpolation between the two neighboring rows; no extrapolation. The matched depth is labeled approximate. AICC2023’s displayed Δdepth and its uncertainty come directly from the source. The diagram uses the inverse match, which differs by less than 0.008 m because of table rounding.

Preserved imperfections

The first 202 AICC2023 gas ages are blank; the first 103 are blank in AICC2012. Four downward steps in AICC2023 gas age and two equal steps in AICC2012 remain as published. No sorting or smoothing repairs them. Matching inverts the ice grid, never the non-monotonic gas grid. Negative BP values remain negative: “present” is 1950, not today.

Scope of the chronology. The authors recommend WD2014-tied timescales for the most recent 60 kyr. Their corrected correspondence table contains 395 volcanic ties, in ka before 2000, with no paired gas ages or uncertainties. This explorer keeps the complete AICC2023 chronology intact and flags its recent part; it does not silently splice scales. The opening and deep examples are older than 60 ka. AICC2012 uncertainty is unavailable in this table and is not inferred.

This page contains no CO₂ concentrations or isotope measurements and makes no claim about their causal timing. It is an instrument for understanding an age–depth model, not for establishing event order or evaluating the model’s scientific validity.
